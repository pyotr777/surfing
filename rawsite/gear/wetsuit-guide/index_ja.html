What to wear, by month and latitude

One table, six regions, twelve months. The gradient is the interesting part.

Japan is long. The six spots on this site span from 43 °N in Hokkaido to 32 °N in Miyazaki — roughly the distance from Milan to Tunis. In August that barely matters: the whole archipelago sits between 22 and 28 °C and almost everyone is in a spring suit or less.

February is where it shows. Miyazaki is still 18 °C, which is comfortable in 3 mm. Shioya, on the Sea of Japan coast of Hokkaido, is 8 °C, which means 5 mm with a hood, boots and gloves, and a session that ends when your hands stop working rather than when the waves do.

Reading the grid

Two patterns are worth noticing. The first is the obvious one: the table gets darker as you move up the rows, because the rows are ordered north to south, exactly like the pins on the map. The second is that the seasons are not symmetrical. Water lags air by about two months, so September is warmer than June everywhere, and the coldest column is February rather than December.

That lag is convenient. On the Pacific side it means typhoon season, the best swell of the year, lands on the warmest water of the year. On the Sea of Japan side it means the opposite: the swell arrives exactly when the water is at its worst.

The grid is generated from csv/wetsuit.csv during the build, including the colour of every cell — the shade is chosen from the number in the cell, so the pattern cannot drift out of step with the data.
