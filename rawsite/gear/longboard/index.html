Longboard

The board that turns a knee-high Tuesday into a session worth having.

At 9′2″ and sixty-five litres a longboard catches essentially anything. The long rail line and a single fin give it stability and a trim speed that no short board can hold on a weak wave, which is why it does not disappear once someone has learned to stand.

It is a different discipline rather than an easier one. Walking the board, holding trim, setting up a nose ride — none of that transfers from shortboarding, and none of it is simple. What it does share with the fish is a tolerance for soft waves: on the small clean days that Kugenuma and Kisakihama produce most of the year, it is the shape that gets the most out of them.

The cost is inertia. Nine feet of foam takes time to redirect, is heavy to carry through a shorebreak, and is genuinely dangerous in a crowd — which is exactly the situation on a warm Shonan weekend.

All five to scale
