Shortboard

Small, thin and demanding — a shape that only makes sense once the wave has some slope in it.

A modern shortboard is around 6′0″ and 28 litres, which for most adults is less flotation than their own body. That is the point and also the problem: with so little volume the board sits low, paddles slowly and will not plane until something pushes it.

What it buys is response. A narrow outline and a squash tail let the board change direction inside the pocket rather than around it, and the thruster fin setup holds through a turn on a face that is already vertical. On Tsurigasaki during a typhoon swell, or Habushiura on a clean spring morning, that is exactly the tool.

On a waist-high summer day it is the wrong tool entirely. Paddling is the honest test: if you are missing waves you would have caught on anything else, the board is not too advanced, it is too small for the conditions.
