Shortboard

Lean and demanding — a shape that comes alive when the wave has real slope.

A modern shortboard is narrow and low in volume. That is the point and also the problem: it sits low in the water, paddles slowly and does not plane until the wave starts doing the work.

What it buys is response. A narrow outline and a squash tail let the board change direction inside the pocket rather than around it, and the thruster fin setup holds through a turn on a face that is already vertical. On Ichinomiya during a typhoon swell, or one of Katsuura’s sandbars on a clean spring morning, that is exactly the tool.

In weak summer surf it is the wrong tool entirely. Paddling is the honest test: if you are missing waves you would have caught on anything else, the board is not too advanced, it is simply the wrong choice for the conditions.
