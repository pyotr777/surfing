Fish

A compact, wide shape that finds speed in soft, weak surf.

A fish is compact, but wide through the middle and full in the nose, with a broad swallow tail. Its extra planing area generates speed when the wave itself is not supplying much.

That combination is unusually well suited to Japanese summers, when the Pacific goes quiet and Shonan produces soft rollers for weeks at a time. A twin-fin setup loosens the tail and makes the board draw long, fast lines instead of tight ones, which is the right instinct on a soft wave.

It gives up authority when the face steepens. The same width that creates speed makes the board skittish once the wave has real power in it, and the swallow tail lets go earlier than a pin. A fish is the second board, not the only one.
