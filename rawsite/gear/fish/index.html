Fish

Half a foot shorter than a shortboard and four litres fatter, which changes everything.

A fish is compact — around 5′6″ — but wide through the middle and thick, with a full nose and a broad swallow tail. The extra planing area is what generates speed when the wave itself is not supplying any.

That combination is unusually well suited to Japanese summers, when the Pacific goes quiet and Shonan produces waist-high rollers for weeks at a time. A twin fin setup loosens the tail and makes the board draw long, fast lines instead of tight ones, which is the right instinct on a soft wave.

It gives up authority when the face steepens. The same width that creates speed makes the board skittish once the wave has real power in it, and the swallow tail lets go earlier than a pin. A fish is the second board, not the only one.
