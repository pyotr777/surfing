Gun

The board you own for three sessions a year and are glad of on all three.

A gun is long — 8′6″ here — but narrow, with a pulled-in nose and a pin tail. The length is there for paddle speed: a big wave moves fast, and getting into it means matching enough of that speed to be picked up before it stands up.

The narrowness does the opposite job. A wide board wants to skip across a steep face; a narrow one with a pin tail digs its rail in and holds a line down a wall that is already breaking behind you. Forty-five litres in that outline floats less than the number suggests, because almost none of it is where a fish would put it.

In Japan this is a Habushiura board, on the handful of days a year when the Izu island exposure turns into something triple overhead. It is genuinely useless the rest of the time, and that is the correct trade.
