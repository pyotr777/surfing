Funboard

The board most people should be on for their second year, and the one most people skip.

A funboard sits between a longboard and a shortboard: about 7′2″, wide, thick, with a rounded outline and a 2+1 fin setup. Forty-six litres is plenty of float, so it paddles into ankle-high waves the way a longboard does.

What separates it from a longboard is that it will still turn. The shorter rail line lets you angle down the face and come back to it, which is the actual skill that has to develop between standing up and surfing. Doing that on a nine-foot board teaches a stance that has to be unlearned later.

Kugenuma is the obvious place for one. It is the classic progression board: forgiving on the small days that make up most of the calendar, and still workable on the head-high ones that arrive with the first typhoon of the season.
