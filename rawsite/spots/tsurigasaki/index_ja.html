Tsurigasaki

Ninety minutes from Tokyo, and the most contested sandbar in the country.

Tsurigasaki is at the southern end of Kujukuri, the 66 km sandy arc that forms most of Chiba's Pacific coast. Locals call the peak Shidashita. In July 2021 it hosted the Olympic surfing competition — the first time the sport appeared at the Games — which is how a working beach town ended up on the world map.

It is a big, open beach break with shifting sandbars, and it earns its reputation on summer and autumn typhoon swell: punchy, contestable peaks, chest to head high on a normal day, fast and steep. The bars rearrange constantly, so which tide and swell direction works is a question with a different answer every few weeks.

Two things catch visitors out. The currents are strong — this is an exposed, shallow, wind-raked coast, and the water moves along it. And the crowd is real: Ichinomiya is close enough to Tokyo for a day trip, and everyone knows it. Not a beginner's beach, despite how gentle the sand looks from the car park.
