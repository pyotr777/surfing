Ikarashi

An ordinary stretch of sand behind a university campus, and one of the most reliable winter beaches on the west coast.

Ikarashi sits behind the dunes west of central Niigata, with a university campus on one side and a bus route on the other. The ordinariness is the point: this is a beach people surf before work, not a destination.

Its season runs backwards compared with the Pacific coast. Between April and October the Sea of Japan is a lake. Then the winter monsoon sets in, north- westerlies blow down from Siberia, and the same water produces something almost every day until March.

What arrives is wind swell rather than ocean swell. The fetch across the Sea of Japan is a few hundred kilometres at most, so the period is short, the faces are steep, and the sets stack up close together — a beach that rewards paddling fitness more than patience. The wind that makes the waves also ruins them, so the good sessions come in the lull after a front, when a light southeasterly turns offshore.
