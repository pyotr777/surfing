Habushiura

Close enough to Tokyo to be a weekend, far enough out to sea to get everything the Pacific sends.

Niijima is one of the Izu Islands, a ferry ride south of Tokyo, and Habushiura is the seven-kilometre sweep of white sand along its eastern side. The water is turquoise; the sandbars are not fixed. Together they produce what a lot of people will tell you is the best beach break in the country.

The exposure is the whole point. Nothing shelters this coast, so it breaks on almost any swell — chest high one week, triple overhead the next, with barrels that hold. Spring is the classic season for clean turquoise tubes, but the wide swell window and the variable winds mean there is usually something all year. A south swell with a west offshore is the combination worth travelling for.

The island's other beaches face different directions, so whichever way the wind blows there is normally somewhere to go. That said, when Habushiura is big there is no gentle version of it — the same exposure that makes it good removes the option of a small day.
