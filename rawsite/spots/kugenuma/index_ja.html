Kugenuma

The gentlest wave on this site, and the busiest.

Shonan is the stretch of Sagami Bay coast an hour from Shinjuku on the Odakyu line, and Kugenuma is its centre of gravity: black volcanic sand, left and right peaks, Enoshima island sitting off the end of the beach and Mount Fuji behind it on a clear winter morning.

The wave is forgiving. It is a soft, rolling beach break at waist to chest high, which is what it is most of the time, and it only turns serious once it goes overhead. Low incoming tide is the classic window; a northerly off the hills is offshore.

Sagami Bay faces south, which limits what can get in — the good days come from south and southeast swell, and typhoon season stretches the usual three- or four-hour wind swell into something that lasts. The cost of all this convenience is company: on a warm weekend the lineup is one of the most crowded in the country, and the etiquette that keeps it functioning is worth learning before you paddle out.
