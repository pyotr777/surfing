Shioya

The northernmost spot on this site, and the only one whose season is measured in snowfall.

Hokkaido has two surf seasons and they barely overlap. The Pacific coast waits for typhoons in late summer. The west coast, facing the Sea of Japan, comes alive in December and stays that way until March: the same low- pressure systems that bury Niseko in powder drag cold air across open water and raise swell on the way.

Shioya is a rivermouth just west of Otaru. The bar shifts with every thaw and every big runoff, which is what rivermouths do — a long hollow left one winter, a shapeless peak the next. A southeasterly comes down off the hills and holds the faces open.

None of this is casual. Air temperatures sit below freezing, snow lands on the water, and the sea itself hovers around 8 °C — cold, but notably warmer than the air, which is why the west coast stays surfable at all. A 5 mm suit with hood, boots and gloves is the floor, and your hands, not the swell, decide when the session ends.
